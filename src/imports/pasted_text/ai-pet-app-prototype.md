Please design a mobile app prototype called “AI Companion Pet”.

This is a low-pressure gamified CGM companion app for young people with Type 1 Diabetes. The app should feel warm, calm, emotional, and companion-like, not clinical or medical. The main concept is a virtual pet living in a small house inside the user’s phone. The pet helps the user understand glucose patterns, make lightweight records, and receive gentle emotional support. It should never score the user, punish the user, compare users, or make the pet sick because of glucose readings.

Overall visual direction:
Use a soft illustrated mobile app style, warm and cozy, like a gentle pet house / scrapbook / journal world. Avoid hospital blue, red warning colours, harsh medical dashboard aesthetics, leaderboards, streaks, badges, or competitive game UI. Use rounded cards, soft shadows, warm cream background, sage green, soft peach, muted brown, warm yellow, and low-saturation colours. The UI should feel like a calm companion space rather than a health tracking dashboard.

App structure:
The home screen is the main hub. It shows the exterior of a small illustrated pet house with the pet visible outside or near the house. The house is the main navigation object. The screen should have:
- A hamburger menu at the top left.
- A small stamp count at the top right.
- The pet as the main visual character.
- A glucose circle at the bottom centre showing colour status, number, and trend arrow.
- A plus button next to the glucose circle for quick logging.
There should be no persistent bottom navigation bar.

Home interaction:
Tapping the pet opens the AI companion chat. Tapping the glucose circle opens Trend Detail. Tapping the plus button opens Quick Log. Tapping the house enters the room space. The house should feel like a daily emotional anchor, not a task dashboard.

Main room concept:
The house interior can be represented as a cozy multi-level pet house. The interior should include three main functional areas:
1. Kitchen / food area
2. Journal and activity area
3. Bedroom / companion area

Kitchen / food area:
This area should look like a warm small kitchen with a fridge, dining table, cabinets, food cards, fruit bowl, bowls, cups, toast, rice bowl, vegetables, and small notes on the fridge. It represents food logging and carb search. The fridge and food table should look tappable.

Journal and activity area:
This area should look like a soft journal room or study corner. It should include a desk, open journal notebook, sticky notes, pens, washi tape, stickers, cork board, paper cards, shelves, and a small fitness corner with yoga mat, dumbbells, jump rope, or exercise ball. It represents quick notes, reflection, and today’s activity.

Bedroom / companion area:
This is the warmest emotional area. It should include a soft bed, pillows, blanket, bedside lamp, rug, wardrobe, pet accessories, postcard wall, small sofa or cushion, window, plants, and warm lighting. The bed or pet area represents AI chat. The wardrobe represents pet outfits. The postcard wall represents travel memories and postcard collection.

Core screens to design:
1. Welcome / onboarding screen with illustrated house and pet.
2. Pet selection screen with 2–3 pet cards and optional naming fields.
3. CGM pairing simulated screen with device brand cards and “connect later”.
4. Home house exterior screen.
5. Room interior screen with kitchen, journal/activity, and bedroom areas.
6. AI companion chat screen.
7. Trend Detail screen.
8. Quick Log screen.
9. Reward feedback screen.
10. World Map / postcard collection screen.
11. Postcard detail screen.
12. Store / wardrobe screen.
13. Settings screen.

AI companion chat:
The chat should feel like talking with a gentle pet, not a doctor or chatbot. Use rounded chat bubbles, pet avatar, warm background, and a visible disclaimer banner: “This companion can help you reflect on patterns, but it does not provide medical advice.” The tone should be calm, supportive, and non-commanding.

Trend Detail:
This screen should help users understand glucose patterns. It should include:
- Current glucose status card with colour, number, and arrow.
- A colour-only toggle for users who do not want to see numbers.
- A simple line chart showing recent glucose trend.
- A dashed 30-minute prediction extension.
- A neutral pattern note.
- A collapsed GMI / HbA1c estimation card with disclaimer.
The design should be informative but not overwhelming.

Quick Log:
This should be very lightweight. Use large tap tags such as Ate, Injected, Exercised, Unwell. Include optional note and optional voice note. Skip and Save buttons must have equal visual weight. Logging should feel optional and non-judgmental.

Reward system:
Use a gentle stamp system. Users receive one stamp only after saving a Quick Log entry. Do not reward opening the app, browsing rooms, or checking trends. No streaks, no scores, no rankings, no deductions. Stamps can unlock postcards, map regions, pet outfits, or room decorations. Rewards should feel like small keepsakes, not performance evaluation.

World map and postcard:
Create a soft illustrated fictional world map with five regions or nations. The map should feel like a travel scrapbook, with soft colours, dotted paths, small landmarks, stamps, tape, and postcard elements. Postcards should look like paper cards with texture, tape, handwritten-style caption, and gentle travel memory feeling. No likes, comments, usernames, or social media metrics.

Companion space:
If included, it should be an anonymous peer space without visible likes, follower counts, rankings, or public comparison. Use category filters and gentle post cards. A “resonance” button can exist, but no count should be shown.

Store and wardrobe:
Design a soft catalog for room decorations and pet outfits. Use fixed prices, owned/unowned states, no mystery boxes, no urgency, no limited-time labels. Items include rugs, cushions, lamps, plants, postcards, scarves, hats, small bags, and pet accessories.

Settings:
Include reminder frequency, notification channel, nudge bubble on/off, peer content on/off, device pairing status, and clear demo data. Reminders should default to low frequency and be adjustable.

Important constraints:
Do not use medical-heavy visuals.
Do not use hospital symbols, red alarms, harsh warning states, leaderboards, streaks, scores, or achievement pressure.
Do not make the pet sad, sick, or worse because of glucose readings or inactivity.
Do not create shame, punishment, or urgency.
The whole app should feel like a low-pressure companion that helps users understand patterns and feel supported.
